    Pre-Submission Referee Report¶

    Paper: The Global Macroeconomic Analysis Projections and Scenarios (GMAPS) Authors: Tobias Adrian, Matyas Farkas, Jesper Linde Date: 2026-07-19 Review Standard: Leading Field Journal

    Overall Assessment¶

    The paper documents the GMAPS, a browser-based platform that produces internally consistent baseline forecasts and conditional scenarios for 55 economies from a single estimated 38-bloc global Bayesian VAR, solved for any user scenario as one precomputed linear fixed point x=(I−M)⁻¹d, with fiscal and external-accounting satellites, soft outside-forecast conditioning, a reactive long-run anchor, and a DSGE policy overlay. Its principal strength is the conditional-forecast global-solve operator — the observation that each bloc's smoother mean is affine in the pins, so cross-country conditional consistency reduces to a single linear fixed point — together with the joint EM completion of a 38-bloc pandemic-missing panel and unusually careful, honest exposition. The single most critical issue is that the paper contains no in-paper quantitative validation: its one committed credibility exhibit (the classical-GVAR GIRF confrontation) is a visible \todo, all out-of-sample evaluation is deferred to a companion paper, the load-bearing ρ(M)=0.98917 near-unit multiplier (23× cumulative world amplification) is never stress-tested, and the external/NFA baseline contradicts its own current account. Compounding this, the headline "4× world spillover" is substantially definitional (the US pins are global commons imposed on every bloc), not an estimated transmission result — a point a referee will raise immediately.

    Preliminary Recommendation: Substantial revision required

    1. Spelling, Grammar & Style¶

    I've read all the files. Below is the copy-editing review, prose only.

    No outright spelling errors were found; proper nouns (Smets–Wouters, Metropolis–Hastings, Villani, Gauss–Seidel, Smith–Galesi, Eurosystem, Macrofinancial) and US spelling conventions (labor, behavior, modeling, parametrization, division of labor) are used consistently. The issues below are grammar, number/consistency, and style.

    Critical Issues¶

    1. Comma splice in the abstract — gmaps_platform_paper.tex:77-80: "Holding the ten-year yield fixed on impact isolates the credit shock, the global solve carries it to every economy and roughly quadruples the world output loss a US-only analysis would show, and a Smets--Wouters counterfactual prices…" Two independent clauses are joined by a bare comma. Replace the first comma with a semicolon or restructure. This is the paper's headline sentence, so it matters.

    2. Signed macro collides with "below baseline" (double negative) — the script-generated constants are inconsistent about whether they carry a sign, and the prose only reads correctly with the unsigned ones.

    3. gmaps:235-236: "growth dipping \TrueGdpGrowthMinPp percentage points below baseline" renders as "dipping -1.86 percentage points below baseline" — the minus sign plus "below baseline" says the opposite of what is meant.
    4. gmaps:259-260: "the net-IIP ratio still slips \ExtScenNfaDevH points below baseline" renders "slips -0.35 points below baseline" — same contradiction.

    5. By contrast gmaps:306 uses the positive \EAGdpTroughPp = 2.11 correctly ("troughs 2.11 percentage points below baseline"), and gmaps:300-301 prepends a manual minus to a positive macro ($-\USGdpTroughGlobPp$). So \TrueGdpGrowthMinPp (-1.86) and \ExtScenNfaDevH (-0.35) embed a sign while \EAGdpTroughPp, \JPGdpTroughPp, \USGdpTroughGlobPp do not. Standardize the constants to unsigned magnitudes and let the prose supply "below baseline."

    6. Subject–verb disagreement — gmaps:571 (App. B): "the seasonal forecast path produced by the filter and by the band simulator agree to numerical precision." Singular "path" with plural "agree." Make it "paths … agree" (two paths are being compared).

    7. Comma splice — sec2_methodology.tex:346-347: "The conditional-forecast operator is the object the tool needs, it is affine in what the user imposes, and affinity is what turns…" First comma splices two independent clauses; use a semicolon.

    8. Malformed comparative — gmaps:263-264: "…which for the United States omits the higher yield it earns on its external assets than it pays on its liabilities…" You cannot "omit the higher yield … than …"; the construction is broken. Rewrite, e.g., "omits the fact that the United States earns a higher yield on its external assets than it pays on its liabilities" or "omits the yield differential between US external assets and liabilities."

    9. Hand-typed number contradicts the paper's own claim — \rho(M)=0.98917 is typed literally three times (sec2:291, sec2:364, sec2:412), yet constants.tex:20 defines \RhoM = 0.989 and it is never used. This violates the stated contract ("Every quoted number is a macro from constants.tex … never hand-typed", header) and the explicit claim at gmaps:615: "nothing is typed by hand." It also creates a precision mismatch (0.98917 vs 0.989). Route these three through a macro; note that constants.tex:2 itself admits the file is "HAND-SEEDED for the skeleton," so the "nothing is typed by hand" sentence is currently false and should be softened until the generator is wired up.

    Minor Issues¶

    • Faulty parallelism after either/or — gmaps:362-363: "a realistic response is either persistent rather than one-off, or it accepts part of the loss." Mixes an adjective phrase with a clause. Fix: "either is persistent rather than one-off, or accepts part of the loss."
    • Dangling modifier — gmaps:309-310: "Treated as a US-only event, world growth dips 0.54 percentage points…" It is the scenario, not world growth, that is treated as US-only.
    • Awkward stacking — gmaps:595-596: "with its seasonal treatment and with and without the shipped long-run anchors." Read: "with its seasonal treatment, both with and without the shipped anchors."
    • Garden-path word order — sec2:363-365: "which makes the global amplification the solve produces powerful…" The predicate adjective lands too far from "makes." Rewrite: "which makes the amplification produced by the solve powerful."
    • Missing comma — sec2:198-200: "and of the foreign variables ROWDEM and ROWINF are masked…" Needs a comma: "and, of the foreign variables, ROWDEM and ROWINF are masked…"
    • Sentence fragment — sec2:451: "A last word on the fiscal side, because it works differently from the rest of the bloc and users notice." No main verb.
    • Awkward "and hold" — sec2:181-182: "no rank and restriction decisions have to be made, and hold, for 38 blocs." Reads as "decisions … hold." Rewrite ("made — and kept").
    • \emph inside math mode — sec2:277: $\emph{affine}$ will not italicize as intended inside $…$; move the emphasis outside the math.
    • Visible TODO in the manuscript — gmaps:591-592: \todo{GIRF figure pending the toolbox estimation run.} plus "will appear … in the next revision." A referee will read this as an incomplete submission.
    • Unhyphenated numeric modifier — "a 100 basis point rise" (gmaps:74, gmaps:158, fig_naive_true.tex:39) should be "a 100-basis-point rise" when attributive (contrast the correctly hyphenated "ten-year", "five-year").
    • Loose count — sec2:348: "a fifty-economy system" understates the 55 served models and does not match the 38 coupled blocs; use one of the exact macros or "fifty-odd."
    • Terminology drift for the stock-flow variable — "stock-flow term" (sec2:179), "stock-flow residual" (sec2:465), "stock-flow adjustment" (sec2:466, eq. 20). Standardize (SFA = "stock-flow adjustment").
    • Same series, two names — "ten-year government yield" (gmaps:227) vs "ten-year Treasury yield" (gmaps:212, 222).
    • Gendered generic pronoun — gmaps:419: "when the user changes her mind." The only gendered pronoun in the paper; prefer "their" for consistency.
    • Informal abbreviation — "applied macro" (gmaps:370) and "macro forecasts" (App. B, gmaps:601); spell out "macroeconomics"/"macroeconomic."
    • Inconsistent capitalization — "the model data base" (gmaps:515) vs the proper name "Macroeconomic Model Data Base" (gmaps:331, 459).
    • Near-verbatim duplication — the "algorithm fine print" footnote (sec2:245-249) and Appendix A paragraph (gmaps:553-556) repeat almost the same sentences; consolidate or cross-reference.
    • Possible latent contradiction — global_constants.tex:9 sets \GlobalSweeps = 1 ("sweeps to converge"), while sec2:296 states "hundreds of sweeps are needed." The macro is currently unused, so nothing renders wrong, but if it is ever cited the two will clash. (Also unused and worth pruning or wiring in: \RhoM, \WorldAmp, \NaiveLrPeakBp, \CFResidualPct, \ExtUsCaAnchor, \ExtUsNfaH, \ExtScenCaDevH, \WUSEA/\WUSMX/\WUSCA/\WUSCN, \LastObsQ, \NowcastQ.)

    Style Patterns to Fix Throughout¶

    • Oxford comma is inconsistent. Present: gmaps:91 ("growth, inflation, and interest rates"), gmaps:318, gmaps:465, sec2:427. Absent: gmaps:135 ("interest rates and financial prices"), gmaps:253 ("exports, imports and exchange rate"), sec2:121 ("credit, housing and, for…"). Pick one (the paper leans serial-comma in most lists) and apply it everywhere.
    • Em-dash spacing is inconsistent. The dominant style is spaced --- (e.g., gmaps:92, gmaps:132, gmaps:107), but the external-accounts paragraph uses unspaced ---: "value---for" and "output---and" (gmaps:253-254) and "stock---the external" (gmaps:261). Normalize.
    • "pre-computed" vs "precomputed." gmaps:277 ("pre-computed operator") against sec2:293, sec2:297 ("precomputed"). Choose one.
    • Intensifier/filler overuse. "exactly" recurs ~8× (gmaps:275, gmaps:406, sec2:433, sec2:438, sec2:462, sec2:399, sec2:447, and more), "simply" ~4× (gmaps:228, gmaps:310, sec2:128, sec2:215), "actually" 3× (gmaps:378, sec2:276, sec2:445). Thin these out; most sentences are stronger without them.
    • Authorial self-flagging / "worth noting" family. "One consequence deserves emphasis" (sec2:139), "All three deserve to be on the table" (sec2:331), "The costs deserve equal daylight" (sec2:359), "a division of labor that deserves to be stated precisely" (gmaps:452), "One choice is worth flagging" (gmaps:262), "we want to state it plainly" (gmaps:366). This is the essayistic register's version of "it is worth noting"; keep one or two, cut the rest.
    • Person/register drift. Second-person "you" ("the model surprises you", gmaps:159; "what you hold still as by what you move", gmaps:161, gmaps:242) sits alongside "we," "a user," "the user," and "a desk economist." A field journal will want the second person removed in favor of the consistent "we"/"the user."
    • Repeated signature phrase. "from a first click to a policy conclusion" appears near-verbatim three times (abstract gmaps:74; gmaps:158; gmaps:185). Vary two of them.
    • Comma-spliced enumerations of independent clauses recur as a stylistic tic: sec2:58-60 ("Each country model is cast…, the number… is determined…, estimation is by…"), sec2:434, sec2:445-447, gmaps:346. Where the items are full clauses, prefer semicolons.
    • Numeric compound modifiers are hyphenated unevenly — "ten-year," "two-year," "five-year," "one-off," "one-click" are hyphenated, but "100 basis point rise" is not. Standardize attributive numeric compounds.

    One structural note beyond prose, flagged for the authors: the coverage table (coverage_table.tex) still lists RU among the 38 coupled blocs, which the constants (\NBlocs=38) and the eigenvalue/\dim x=2,360 arithmetic all assume — internally consistent as written, but confirm RU is intended in this vintage before submission.

    2. Internal Consistency & Cross-Reference Verification¶

    I have completed a full cross-check of every number against its source, all cross-references, citations, and terminology. Findings below.

    Critical Inconsistencies¶

    C1. The naive policy-rate peak (44 bp) contradicts its own script-generated figure data (≈42 bp). - figs/data/us_constants.tex:3 defines \newcommand{\NaivePolPeakBp}{44} and the body quotes it: "the policy rate climbs \NaivePolPeakBp{} basis points at its peak" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:213). - But figs/data/us_spread.csv (same script, us_spread_cf.mjs, identical timestamp) has the naive policy path pol_n peaking at 0.4192 (q4) → 41.9 ≈ 42 bp, not 44. The maximum over the whole column is 0.4192; no value reaches 0.435+. - This is a text-vs-exhibit mismatch: Figure~\ref{fig:naivetrue}'s policy-rate panel plots pol_n (peaks just above 0.4), so a reader comparing the text ("44 bp") to the figure sees a disagreement. Either the constant is stale relative to the CSV or the peak extraction in the script has a bug. The 10-year impact (\NaiveLrImpactBp{}=69, csv lr_n q1=0.6871→69) checks out; only the policy peak is off. Verify/regenerate.

    C2. The body asserts a classical-GVAR confrontation that the appendix admits does not yet exist (plus a live \todo in the PDF). - sec2_methodology.tex:366 states, present tense: "Appendix~B confronts the resulting responses with the impulse responses of a classical GVAR estimated on the same data." - But app:cred (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:590-592) says the exhibit "is being produced with the restarted evaluation (below) and will appear in this appendix in the next revision," followed by a rendered \todo{GIRF figure pending the toolbox estimation run.} (:592). The \todo macro (:52) prints a red bracketed note into the compiled PDF. - For a top-field submission this is both an internal overstatement (a claimed comparison that isn't there) and a submission-readiness defect (visible TODO). The forward-reference at gmaps_platform_paper.tex:371 ("Appendix B returns to this distinction in more detail") is fine, since the interpretation distinction is in app:cred; only the confrontation claim overstates the current content.

    Cross-Reference Errors¶

    X1. "The full roster appears in Appendix C" — Appendix C does not contain the roster. - sec2_methodology.tex:429: "The full roster appears in Appendix~C." - Appendix C (app:repro, gmaps_platform_paper.tex:608-611) contains only the bloc-level coverage table (n, sample, seasonal, fiscal) and explicitly redirects the per-variable roster elsewhere: "the per-variable rosters, sources, and transformations for all \NModels{} economies are in the online manual, and for the United States in \citet{CrumpEusepiGiannoneQianSbordone2025}." The claim should say the roster is in the online manual, not Appendix C.

    X2. Hardcoded appendix letters instead of \ref{} (currently correct but fragile). - "Appendix~B" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:371, sec2_methodology.tex:366) and "Appendix~C" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:188, sec2_methodology.tex:429) are typed literally rather than \ref{app:cred} / \ref{app:repro}. They resolve correctly given the current A/B/C ordering (app:model/app:cred/app:repro), but any reordering silently breaks them. Not an error today; a hazard worth converting to \ref.

    All other cross-references verified correct: every \ref/\eqref (Figures ui-cond, naivetrue, external, ui-ea, global, cf, ui-cf, pesaran, gbvar; Tables coverage, gvar-vs; eqs varx, starsclassical, stack, mffmbloc, partition, rowdem–rowfin, wtilde, ss, fixedpoint, debt, softpin, anchor, delta; Sections intro/method/example/solve/estimation/classical/counterfactual/conclusion) points to an existing target that shows what is claimed. The two TikZ schematics (figs/tikz/fig_pesaran.pdf, fig_gbvar.pdf) both exist.

    Terminology Drift¶

    T1. \RhoM (0.989) is defined but never used; \rho(M)=0.98917 is hand-typed three times — a direct violation of the paper's own binding style contract. - constants.tex:20 defines \newcommand{\RhoM}{0.989} "updated ONLY by regeneration," and gmaps_platform_paper.tex:8 states the contract: "Every quoted number is a macro from constants.tex (script-generated; never hand-typed)." - Yet 0.98917 is hardcoded at sec2_methodology.tex:291, :363, and :412 (the comparison table), and \RhoM appears 0 times. The value is not a contradiction (0.98917 rounds to 0.989), but three hand-typed copies of a number that is supposed to live in a macro is exactly the maintenance hazard the contract forbids, and the two representations (0.989 vs 0.98917) can drift apart on the next regeneration. Route all three through a macro (a precise \RhoMexact or widen \RhoM).

    T2. "\NModels economies" (55) mildly double-counts the euro area. Abstract (:69) and intro (:134) call \NModels{}=55 a count of "economies," but constants.tex:10 defines it as 55 country models = \NBlocs(38) + \NMembers(17). Since the euro-area aggregate is one of the 38 blocs and its 17 members are counted separately, "55 economies" counts the euro area both as an aggregate and via its members (distinct economies ≈ 54). "55 models" is precise; "55 economies" is loose. Minor.

    No drift found in the terms the brief flagged: the prose uses GMAPS consistently (the "G-BVAR" string survives only as the internal label sec:gbvar, never in text); net IIP / net international investment position is used uniformly in prose (the "NFA/Nfa" spelling lives only in invisible macro names and CSV headers); and "anchor" means the long-run anchor throughout the prose (the external nowcast "anchor" appears only in macro identifiers). These are clean.

    Minor Inconsistencies¶

    • Sign/wording double-negatives from signed macros. gmaps_platform_paper.tex:236 renders "growth dipping −1.86 percentage points below baseline" (\TrueGdpGrowthMinPp{}=-1.86 already carries the minus), and :260-261 renders "the net-IIP ratio still slips −0.35 points below baseline" (\ExtScenNfaDevH{}=-0.35). "Dipping/slips −X below baseline" is a double negative. The same \TrueGdpGrowthMinPp macro is used correctly at :300 ("from −1.86 … to −2.37"), which is what exposes the inconsistency: either drop the sign in the macro or drop "below baseline" at :236/:260.

    • Forecast origin/nowcast date is never stated, and its macros are unused. \LastObsQ(2026Q1) and \NowcastQ(2026Q2) are defined (constants.tex:16-17) but used 0 times; the example leans heavily on "the nowcast quarter" (:194, :349, all figure captions) without ever dating it, while the coverage table and \USSampleEnd report the estimation sample ending 2025Q4. The 2025Q4-estimation-vs-2026Q1-origin shift is coherent in the tool but invisible and unexplained in the paper; a reader cannot tell whether pins land in 2026Q1 or 2026Q2. Consider stating the origin once.

    • \GlobalSweeps{}=1 conflicts with the "hundreds of sweeps" footnote. global_constants.tex:9 labels it "Gauss-Seidel sweeps to converge = 1," while sec2_methodology.tex:296-297 says Gauss–Seidel at ρ=0.989 needs "hundreds of sweeps." Harmless today (the macro is unused), but the data file is internally contradictory with the text.

    • LenzaPrimiceri2022 is cited for a treat-as-missing choice that is not their method. sec2_methodology.tex:196-198 cites them for masking 2020Q2–Q4, but Lenza–Primiceri's method is volatility rescaling, not treat-as-missing. Citing them for the general "estimate a VAR after March 2020" problem is defensible, but as written it can read as attributing the missing-data method to them. Consider "in the spirit of the post-2020 estimation problem raised by …" or citing the data-augmentation lineage already present (DurbinKoopman2002, TannerWong1987).

    • Housekeeping — defined-but-unused macros: \WorldAmp, \WUSEA/\WUSMX/\WUSCA/\WUSCN, \NaiveLrPeakBp, \CFResidualPct, \ExtUsCaAnchor, \ExtUsNfaH, \ExtScenCaDevH are all defined but cited 0 times in prose. Not errors, but note \WorldAmp(23.1×, cumulative) measures a different quantity than the "roughly four times"/"quadruples" trough ratio the text actually uses (2.35/0.54≈4.35×) — keep them from being conflated if either is later surfaced.

    Numbers that verify clean (spot-checked against CSVs)¶

    All of the following match their script-generated sources exactly: \NaiveLrImpactBp=69 (lr_n q1=0.6871); two-pin GDP trough \TrueGdpTroughPct=1.01 at q4 (gdp_scen_lvl=−1.0103), \TrueGdpGrowthMinPp=−1.86 (gdp_t q1); \DebtGapHPp=1.9 (debt 124.29−122.35 at q20); \CFEasingBp=281 (pol_cf−pol_scen at q1=−2.815); \CFResidualPct=0.00 (gdp_cf_lvl q4=−0.0000); \USGdpTroughGlobPp=2.37, \EAGdpTroughPp=2.11, \JPGdpTroughPp=2.38, \WorldTroughUsoPp=0.54, \WorldTroughGlobPp=2.35, \WorldTroughJointPp=2.62 (all match global_spread.csv/world.csv troughs); external anchors -3.66/3.61/3.99, seed -66.8, \ExtScenCaPeakPp=0.25, \ExtScenNfaDevH=−0.35. \dim x = 38·3·20+4·20 = 2360 is correct. Bloc/count consistency all holds: coverage table has exactly 38 rows (=\NBlocs), 25 "yes" fiscal (=\NFiscal), 18 seasonal (=\NSeasonal); 38+17=55 (=\NModels); US 1998Q3–2025Q4 = 110 quarters (=\USSampleT), n=40 (=\NUSVars, and "the other thirty-eight variables" = 40−2 pins). The abstract's "roughly quadruples" matches the body's "roughly four times" (4.35×). Every citation key used resolves to a references.bib entry — no missing bibliography items, including the two easily-confused AdolfsonLaseenLindeVillani2007 and AdolfsonLindeVillani2007.

    3. Unsupported Claims & Identification Integrity¶

    The paper is, on the whole, unusually disciplined about the boundary this review polices: it repeatedly and correctly distinguishes the reduced-form conditional forecast ("a statement about likelihood, not about cause," sec2_methodology.tex:578; gmaps_platform_paper.tex:325-332, 366-371, 450-479) from the structural DSGE overlay, draws the counterfactual as a separately-labeled line, and defers both the out-of-sample horse-race and the classical-GVAR confrontation to a companion paper rather than claiming them as done. It does not claim "first." That restraint is real and should be credited. The findings below are where specific claims still outrun the exhibits, plus one load-bearing math inconsistency a referee will catch immediately.

    Internal consistency & math (blocking)¶

    • The debt identity has an inconsistent unit conversion (sec2_methodology.tex:456-462). Equation \eqref{eq:debt} is d_t = d_{t-1}(1+i_t/400)/(1+g_t/100) − pb_t/4 + s_t/4, with the definitions (line 461-462) stating i_t is "percent per annum" and g_t is "nominal GDP growth (percent, quarter-on-quarter annualized)." Both i_t and g_t are therefore in the same units (annualized percent), yet the interest term divides by 400 (correct: annual %→quarterly fraction) while the growth term divides by 100. For a one-quarter recursion the annualized growth must also be /400 (quarterly log-change ≈ g_t/400). As printed, the denominator grows ~4× too fast, biasing the level of the debt ratio downward every quarter. This is exactly the "4·diff = q/q annualized" scaling trap; the interest term (EFFI = 400×quarterly-interest/debt) confirms the /400 convention is the intended one. Either g_t/100 should be g_t/400, or g_t is mislabeled as annualized. Because the fiscal block's rhetorical force is that "the debt line moved because accounting moved it" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:248, 466-467), the printed identity has to be exactly right. (The headline \DebtGapHPp{}=1.9pp is a scenario-minus-baseline difference, in which a common multiplicative denominator bias largely cancels, so the reported number is probably robust even though the level path is not — but the equation as displayed is wrong.)

    • The "nothing typed by hand" reproducibility claim is not yet true (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:615-617, gmaps_platform_paper.tex:8). The style contract and Appendix C both assert "every number quoted in the text is generated by scripts … nothing is typed by hand." But constants.tex:2-4 states it is "HAND-SEEDED for the skeleton … Step 3 of the plan replaces this file with a SCRIPT-GENERATED version." So \NBlocs, \NMembers, \RhoM, \NFiscal, etc. are currently hand-seeded. Relatedly, \rho(M)=0.98917 is hard-typed three times in prose (sec2_methodology.tex:291, 366, 412) instead of using the defined macro \RhoM (=0.989, constants.tex:20), which is left unused. A referee who opens the repo will find the reproducibility claim only partially honored; either regenerate constants.tex before submission or soften the claim.

    Causal / claim overreach¶

    • "A US credit event is a world credit event in this model, by design rather than by accident" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:305), with EA/JP GDP troughs "magnitudes comparable to the US's own" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:306-307, global_constants.tex: EA −2.11, JP −2.38, US −2.37). This is transparently attributed to design (the pinned variables are the global prices carried by every bloc), which is honest. But the near-symmetry — Japan's GDP falling slightly more than the US's from a US-originated shock — is a mechanical consequence of broadcasting the four US financial prices onto every bloc with no attenuation, amplified by ρ(M)=0.98917. The text never cautions the reader that these cross-country magnitudes are therefore not an empirical estimate of international transmission; they are what the global-price-broadcast assumption plus a near-unit multiplier mechanically produce. State this where the magnitudes are reported, not only obliquely in the costs section.

    • "everything it shows comes from one estimated model" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:69). The abstract's own later sentences contradict this: the priced policy response is a Smets–Wouters overlay (a different, structural model), and the fiscal/external paths are accounting identities, not BVAR output. Qualify to "everything in the baseline and scenarios," since the counterfactual and the satellites are explicitly not from the estimated BVAR.

    • The counterfactual's causal reach is narrower than "adjust coherently around the structural core" implies (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:471-476). After the SW07 deltas for r/π/y are imposed as pins, the other thirty-odd variables move via the reduced-form smoother — i.e., by conditional correlation, not by the DSGE's structural propagation. The causal interpretation strictly attaches only to the three imposed core deltas; the derived responses of everything else are still likelihood statements. The paper elsewhere is scrupulous about this distinction and should extend it here. (Also worth a line: SW07 is a closed-economy model, so the priced easing omits the exchange-rate channel the rest of the tool carries.)

    Generalization issues¶

    • The entire quantitative case is one example and, for the global claims, three displayed blocs. "Every economy in the dropdown now shows its own response" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:279) but only US/EA/JP + a world aggregate are shown (fig_global.tex).     Mathematical Errors¶

    1. Debt identity — the growth term is mislabeled as "annualized" (substantive; replication hazard). sec2_methodology.tex:456-462, Eq. eq:debt: $\(d_t = d_{t-1}\frac{1+i_t/400}{1+g_t/100} - \frac{pb_t}{4} + \frac{s_t}{4}\)$ with prose "\(g_t\) nominal GDP growth (percent, quarter-on-quarter annualized)" (line 462). The interest term divides by 400 (annualized percent → quarterly fraction), so for internal consistency the growth term's /100 requires \(g_t\) to be the non-annualized quarterly growth in percent. I confirmed against the code: app/src/lib/compute/fiscalAccounting.ts:12 documents gNomQ : % per QUARTER … (e.g. 1 = 1% q/q), line 105 sets gQ = gNomQ/100, and line 104 sets iQ = effAdj/400. So the formula is correct but the verbal label "annualized" is wrong — it should read "quarter-on-quarter, in percent (not annualized)." This matters: the model's displayed GDP growth uses the annualized \(4\Delta(100\ln)\) convention (line 126), so a reader taking \(g_t\) off the chart and plugging into \(1+g_t/100\) would overstate the growth adjustment ~4×. Fix the word, not the equation.

    2. "unique solution if and only if \(\rho(M)<1\)" is imprecise (Eq. eq:fixedpoint). sec2_methodology.tex:290: "with a unique solution if and only if \(\rho(M)<1\)." The linear system \(x=(I-M)^{-1}d\) has a unique solution iff \(1\notin\mathrm{spec}(M)\) (i.e. \(\det(I-M)\neq0\)). \(\rho(M)<1\) is sufficient for uniqueness — and it is the correct condition for the Neumann series / the Gauss–Seidel iteration to converge and for the multiplier to be stable — but it is not necessary for uniqueness, so the "only if" is false as stated. Given \(\rho(M)=0.989\) sits just under one, the distinction is worth stating correctly: recommend "…has the unique solution \((I-M)^{-1}d\) whenever \(1\notin\mathrm{spec}(M)\); \(\rho(M)<1\) additionally guarantees the iterative (Gauss–Seidel) path converges to it."

    (Tool/platform-documentation paper, adapted framework. All files read in full: gmaps_platform_paper.tex, sec2_methodology.tex, constants.tex, the four figs/data/*_constants.tex, the four fig_*.tex, and coverage_table.tex.)

    Part 1 — Central Contribution¶

    One-sentence claim. The GMAPS is a browser-based, second-fast platform that produces internally consistent baseline forecasts and conditional scenarios for 55 economies from a single estimated global Bayesian VAR — 38 coupled blocs linked by three trade-weighted foreign variables and four global financial prices, estimated jointly across ragged/pandemic-missing data by an EM/data-augmentation loop, and solved for any scenario as one precomputed linear fixed point x=(I−M)⁻¹d — with fiscal and external accounting satellites, soft outside-forecast conditioning, a reactive Villani-style long-run anchor, and a DSGE policy overlay layered on top.

    Is it genuinely new vs. the closest priors? Mixed, and the paper's own framing ("the contribution is the integration", gmaps_platform_paper.tex:169) is the honest one. - Not new: a Bayesian GVAR with Minnesota shrinkage in levels instead of cointegration already exists — Crespo Cuaresma–Feldkircher–Huber and the BGVAR package. The paper positions only against the classical cointegrated GVAR (Pesaran/Dees, sec2_methodology.tex:17–102) and never cites the Bayesian GVAR line. The claim of novelty on the econometric core (sec2:174–176, "we discipline it with shrinkage") is undercut by this omission and cannot stand until it is confronted head-on. - Genuinely new / valuable: (i) the conditional-forecast global operator — the observation that each bloc's smoother mean is affine in the pins, so cross-country conditional consistency is a single linear fixed point (I−M)⁻¹d (sec2:264–297). GVAR practice ships GIRFs from G⁻¹, not a coupled conditional solve; this is the paper's sharpest methodological idea. (ii) The joint EM completion of a 38-bloc unbalanced panel with pandemic-as-missing and a Jacobi star fixed point (sec2:236–262) is a real, non-trivial estimation contribution. (iii) The engineering integration (precomputed inverse → interactive world scenarios in ~1s) is genuinely useful and, for a policy institution, non-obvious.

    Closest prior: the ECB multi-country/ECB-BASE model documentation (AngeliniBokanCiccarelliLalikZimic2025, cited and explicitly held up as the genre standard, gmaps_platform_paper.tex:119–124) for the institutional-tool comparison; and Bayesian GVAR (Feldkircher–Huber, uncited) for the econometric comparison.

    Rating: Significant as a platform + conditional-solve operator; Incremental on the pure econometrics. But this rating is conditional — as written the paper does not yet demonstrate the contribution quantitatively (see Part 2), so on delivered evidence it currently sits at the Incremental/Significant boundary.

    Part 2 — Credibility¶

    Does the paper convince that the tool does what it claims? The mechanics are described carefully and honestly; the behavioral claims are largely unvalidated and, in two places, internally inconsistent with the shipped constants.

    Threat 1 — No quantitative validation is actually in the paper. The single credibility exhibit the paper "commit[s] to" — the classical-GVAR GIRF confrontation on the same data — is a placeholder: "The exhibit is being produced … and will appear in this appendix in the next revision. \todo{GIRF figure pending the toolbox estimation run}" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:590–592). The out-of-sample evaluation is likewise deferred: "Results go to a companion paper; this one documents the platform" (:604; also :510–513). So a tool paper whose headline is a novel global solve contains zero external benchmarking of that solve. A skeptical seminar's first question — "how do I know these numbers are right and not an artifact?" — has no answer in the current draft.

    Threat 2 — ρ(M)=0.98917 is close to unity, and the amplification it produces is enormous. sec2:291,366,412 report ρ(M)=0.98917; the paper concedes this "makes the global amplification the solve produces powerful and places weight on the star equations that generate it" (sec2:363–366). The generated constants make the stakes concrete: the cumulative world amplification is \WorldAmp = 23.1× (global_constants.tex:8) — a 23-fold blow-up of a US credit shock. No sensitivity to ρ is shown, and a re-estimation that nudged ρ above 1 would make (I−M)⁻¹ non-existent. This near-unit multiplier is the single biggest credibility risk and is essentially unaddressed.

    Threat 3 — the "4× world spillover" is partly definitional, not discovered. The two pinned variables (US 10y, US Baa) are two of the four global commons carried as regressors in every bloc (sec2:124–129). So EA and Japan trough at −2.11 and −2.38 pp — comparable to the US's own −2.37 (global_constants.tex:2–4) — largely because the shock is mechanically imposed on their own equations, not because it propagates through trade. The paper is admirably explicit ("A US credit event is a world credit event in this model, by design", gmaps_platform_paper.tex:304), but the abstract's framing — "the global solve carries it to every economy and roughly quadruples the world output loss" (:78) — reads as a spillover finding when it is substantially a consequence of the common-commons construction. A US→EA GDP spillover as large as the US domestic effect is far outside standard empirical GVAR estimates, and a referee will say so.

    Threat 4 — the NFA baseline is internally inconsistent with its own current account. The US seed is \ExtUsNfaSeed = −66.8% of GDP and the baseline net-IIP endpoint is \ExtUsNfaH = −55.4% at 5y (nfa_constants.tex:4–5) — an 11-point improvement — while the model runs a current account of \ExtUsCaAnchor = −5.66% (:3). A persistent −5.7% CA cannot coexist with an improving NFA/GDP unless the flow is not being accumulated: −66.8 rescaled by ~22% nominal growth over 5y is −54.9%, i.e. the endpoint is almost exactly the pure denominator effect with zero net flow contribution. Either the roll-forward drops the CA flow or the reported −5.66% CA is not the flow used. Compounding this, the balance-of-payments/NFA identity is never written down — the paper writes the debt identity \eqref{eq:debt} (sec2:456–462) but only refers to the external identity (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:250–268, fig_external.tex:26–30). For a paper that stakes the "external satellite" as a headline exhibit, the identity must be on the page and the baseline reconciled. The authors flag the CA-level return issue (:262–268) but not this stock-flow tension.

    Threat 5 — the frozen-companion approximation is the shipped default, unquantified. "the smoother's companion is held at the previous pass's posterior mean … An exact data-augmentation pass … is available behind a flag as a final polish" (sec2:245–249, gmaps_platform_paper.tex:553–556). The shipped model is therefore the approximate one; the paper never quantifies how far the exact-DA solution differs. For an estimator paper this gap must be closed with a number.

    Threat 6 — smaller internal inconsistencies that a careful referee will catch. - Style-contract violation + precision mismatch on ρ. The build contract says "Every quoted number is a macro from constants.tex … never hand-typed" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:8), yet ρ(M)=0.98917 is hand-typed three times in sec2 while constants.tex:20 defines \RhoM = 0.989. The macro is defined but not used, and at a different precision. - "spread barely changed" contradicts the numbers. The naive case pins Baa +100 and returns 10y \NaiveLrImpactBp = 69 bp (us_constants.tex:2), so the spread widens by 31 bp — a third of the shock — yet the text says "the spread between corporate and government yields barely changed" (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:223). - Debt identity vs. its variable definition. In \eqref{eq:debt} the interest term is i_t/400 (annual %→quarterly) but the growth term is (1+g_t/100), while g_t is defined as "nominal GDP growth (percent, quarter-on-quarter annualized)" (sec2:462). An annualized g should enter as /400, not /100; as written the two conversions are inconsistent (the label or the formula is wrong). This is exactly the "conventions that matter and are easy to get wrong" the paper warns about (gmaps_platform_paper.tex:471). - \GlobalSweeps = 1 (global_constants.tex:9, labeled "Gauss-Seidel sweeps to converge") directly contradicts the paper's own footnote that "hundreds of sweeps are needed" at this ρ (sec2:296–297). Unused in the text, but a generated constant that is simply wrong. - IL sample ends 2024Q4 (coverage_table.tex:37), the only bloc not reaching 2025Q4; unexplained.

    What a skeptical seminar would say: "You've built an elegant and honest instrument, but you've shown me its plumbing, not that its water is clean. Your world multiplier rests on a spectral radius of 0.989 and a 23× cumulative amplification; your cross-country 'spillovers' are as large as domestic effects because you impose the same financial prices everywhere; your external satellite's baseline improves while running a 5.7% deficit; and every validation you promise is in a different paper."

    Part 3 — Required analyses (blockers)¶

    1. Put the classical-GVAR GIRF confrontation in the paper. It is the one exhibit the paper commits to (:585–592) and it is a TODO. Without it there is no external check on the global solve. This is the top blocker.
    2. Ship a minimal out-of-sample slice, not a forward reference. At least one horizon/one target set: GMAPS (with and without anchors) vs. random walk, AR(1), closed-economy BVAR, and the classical GVAR (the benchmark set the paper itself names, :594–604). A tool paper can defer the full horse-race, but "trust us, it's in the companion paper" is not sendable to a leading journal.
    3. ρ(M) robustness. Report how ρ(M), the world trough, and \WorldAmp (23.1×) move under perturbations of the star equations / weights, and confirm the operator stays invertible across estimation vintages. The near-unit multiplier is the paper's load-bearing wall and is currently untested.
    4. Write the external/NFA identity and reconcile the baseline. Put the balance-of-payments recursion on the page next to \eqref{eq:debt}, and explain how a −5.66% CA is consistent with a baseline NFA that improves from −66.8% to −55.4% (nfa_constants.tex:3–5). If the CA flow is not accumulated, that is a bug to fix, not a caption to hedge.
    5. Quantify the frozen-companion approximation. One table: shipped (frozen) vs. exact-DA on the canonical conditional forecast and on ρ(M)/the world trough. If the gap is negligible, say so with a number; if not, it changes the headline exhibits.

    Suggested analyses (strengtheners)

    • Position explicitly against Bayesian GVAR (Feldkircher–Huber BGVAR): what does the EM completion + conditional-solve operator add beyond an existing Bayesian GVAR? This is the difference between "Significant" and "Incremental."
    • Decompose the world response into the trade channel vs. the global-commons channel, so the reader can see how much of the "4×" is genuine spillover vs. imposed common shock (the paper gestures at this in prose, :296–307, but numbers would settle it).
    • A coverage/calibration check on the shipped uncertainty bands (do the posterior bands attain nominal coverage on held-out data?), since bands are advertised throughout.
    • Show the reactive anchor's cost: because it "moves the mean and nothing else" (:444), it is not a Villani steady-state prior (which would tighten long-run bands). One figure contrasting the reactive re-centering with an actual steady-state-prior re-estimation would make the honesty precise.
    • A one-figure sanity check that the DSGE-delta overlay + re-run smoother produces a coherent object — the SW07 general-equilibrium co-movements differ from the BVAR's, and "laying the delta on top then re-running the smoother" (:472–477) mixes two covariance structures.

    Part 4 — Literature positioning¶

    Right citations, well used. The scenario/GaR frame (Adrian et al. 2019/2020/2025), the classical GVAR lineage (Pesaran–Schuermann–Weiner, Dees et al., Chudik–Pesaran, di Mauro–Pesaran, Smith–Galesi toolbox, Pesaran–Shin GIRF), the Bayesian-VAR machinery (Giannone 2015, Doan–Litterman–Sims, Litterman, Sims–Zha, Waggoner–Zha, Banbura–Giannone–Lenza, Durbin–Koopman, Tanner–Wong, Dempster–Laird–Rubin, Lenza–Primiceri), Villani 2009 for the anchor, Antolín-Díaz et al. for the causal/conditional distinction, MMB (Wieland et al.) and Smets–Wouters for the overlay, and the institutional comparators (Vitek IMF-GMM, Adrian et al. IPF, ECB multi-country, Darracq-Paries, Adolfson et al., Lindé–Smets–Wouters) are all appropriate and largely deployed correctly.

    Missing majors. - Bayesian GVAR — the closest econometric prior — is absent. Crespo Cuaresma–Feldkircher–Huber; Huber (2016) density forecasting with Bayesian GVARs + SV; the BGVAR package. This is the omission: the paper claims shrinkage-in-levels as its distinguishing move against cointegration (sec2:172–190) without acknowledging that Bayesian GVARs already do exactly that. Non-negotiable to add and confront. - Pesaran–Schuermann–Smith (2009), "Forecasting … with Global VARs" (IJF) — the canonical GVAR forecast-evaluation paper and the natural benchmark design for the promised OOS. - Bańbura–Giannone–Reichlin (2010), "Large Bayesian VARs" — the cornerstone for the large-BVAR + conditional-forecast approach; only the 2015 conditional-forecast piece is cited. - Minor: for the conditional-forecast-as-tilting interpretation, Robertson–Tallman–Whiteman (relative entropy) and Cogley–Morozov–Sargent would round out the "outside forecast as data" framing (:387–411); for the DSGE-VAR hybrid, Del Negro–Schorfheide.

    Best framing. Lead with the two-comparator structure the paper already has (classical GVAR on one side, ECB institutional-tool documentation on the other) but insert Bayesian GVAR as the third leg and state precisely what the conditional-solve operator + EM completion + interactivity add on top of it. The "we keep the GVAR skeleton, replace weak exogeneity twice and cointegration with shrinkage" story (sec2:299–313, Table tab:gvar-vs) is genuinely good and should survive the revision intact.

    Part 5 — Journal fit + recommendation¶

    Realistic targets, in order. 1. International Journal of Central Banking (IJCB) — best fit. It publishes exactly this genre (policy-model documentation, BVAR/DSGE tools, institutional forecasting infrastructure), tolerates "documentation" papers, and the IMF-authorship + policy framing land well. Highest probability of acceptance after the Part-3 blockers. 2. International Journal of Forecasting (IJF) — strong fit iff the OOS evaluation is brought into the paper; IJF is the home of GVAR forecasting (Pesaran–Schuermann–Smith) and of tool/method papers with real forecast validation. Without the horse-race, it will bounce. 3. Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control / Economic Modelling — plausible secondary homes for the computational-platform angle. 4. JAE / JBES — the most demanding econometrics targets. In current form these are desk-rejects: they require methodological novelty plus validation, and the novelty is partly pre-empted by Bayesian GVAR while the validation is deferred to another paper.

    Recommendation: Revise before sending. The writing is unusually clear, the mechanics are documented to a high standard, and the conditional-solve operator + EM completion are real contributions. But the paper cannot go to referees at a leading journal while its one committed quantitative exhibit is a \todo, all OOS validation lives in a "companion paper," the load-bearing ρ(M)=0.989/23× amplification is untested, and the NFA baseline contradicts its own current account.

    Concrete path to sendable. (a) Land the classical-GVAR GIRF confrontation and a minimal OOS slice in Appendix B. (b) Add a ρ(M) robustness table and confirm invertibility across vintages. (c) Write the external/NFA identity and reconcile the −66.8→−55.4 baseline against the −5.66% CA. (d) Cite and confront Bayesian GVAR (Feldkircher–Huber) explicitly. (e) Fix the internal-consistency defects: route ρ through \RhoM, correct the debt-identity g_t/100 vs. "annualized" mismatch, fix "spread barely changed", and drop/repair \GlobalSweeps=1. With (a)–(e), send to IJCB (or IJF if the horse-race is complete).

    Part 6 — Referee questions (targeting the weakest points)¶

    1. ρ(M)=0.98917 and a 23.1× cumulative world multiplier: how stable is the spectral radius across re-estimation vintages and across perturbations of the star weights, and how do the headline world troughs move as ρ→1? At what ρ does the shipped (I−M)⁻¹ cease to exist, and how close are you to it?
    2. Is the "4×" a spillover or a construction? Since the two pinned variables are global commons carried in every bloc, decompose the euro-area/Japan −2.1/−2.4 pp troughs into the trade channel vs. the direct common-price channel. What fraction survives if the pinned prices are not broadcast to the other blocs' own equations?
    3. External satellite: please write the balance-of-payments/NFA identity explicitly and reconcile it — how does the baseline net-IIP improve from −66.8% to −55.4% of GDP over five years while the model runs a −5.66% current account? Is the CA flow being accumulated into the stock?
    4. Frozen-companion approximation: the shipped model holds the companion at the previous pass's posterior mean. Quantify the difference between the shipped solution and the exact data-augmentation solution on the canonical conditional forecast and on ρ(M) — is the approximation innocuous for the headline exhibits?
    5. Reactive anchor: because it re-centers the mean and leaves the bands unchanged (:444), in what sense is this "the reactive counterpart" of Villani's steady-state prior rather than a cosmetic level shift? What is lost relative to a prior that actually informs the estimated long-run?
    6. DSGE overlay coherence: you lay a Smets–Wouters IRF delta on the reduced-form scenario and re-run the smoother. Why is the combined path internally coherent given that the DSGE's general-equilibrium co-movements differ from the BVAR's covariance? Show that the re-smoothed bloc is not double-counting or fighting the imposed delta.
    7. Positioning: what does the GMAPS add over an existing Bayesian GVAR (Feldkircher–Huber BGVAR) — is the novelty the conditional-solve operator and EM completion specifically, and can you demonstrate that with a like-for-like comparison rather than only against the classical cointegrated GVAR?
