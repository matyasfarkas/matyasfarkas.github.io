    Feasibility report — extending GMAPS's foreign block toward the CCFH 6-counterpart mirror¶

    Date: 2026-07-19 · Status: exploration only, NOT implemented · Scope: whether GMAPS could add the foreign (weakly-exogenous) counterparts that Crespo Cuaresma, Feldkircher & Huber (2016, JAE) carry, and at what coverage/cost.

    1. The gap¶

    CCFH give each country a near-full foreign mirror (their eq. 4.2): x*ᵢₜ = (y*, Δp*, eq*, i_s*, i_l*, tc*) — trade-weighted foreign counterparts of six of their seven domestic variables (output, inflation, real equity, short rate, long rate, total credit; the real exchange rate is the one variable not mirrored) — plus oil as a global control.

    GMAPS carries only three trade-weighted stars — ROWDEM (≈ y*), ROWINF (≈ Δp*), ROWFIN (≈ i_s*) — and routes financial linkage through four US-originated global commons (oil, US 10y GLB_USLR, US Baa GLB_USBAA, US equity GLB_USEQ) that every bloc carries directly rather than as trade-weighted aggregates.

    So GMAPS already has 3 of CCFH's 6 (output, inflation, short rate). The candidates to add are the three foreign financials: eq* (foreign equity), i_l* (foreign long rate), tc* (foreign total credit).

    2. Coverage — the domestic base each foreign star would average over¶

    A foreign star for variable X is a trade-weighted average of partners' domestic X, so it is only well-defined where partners actually carry X. Scanning all 55 served blocs:

    CCFH counterpart domestic coverage verdict
    y* (output) 55/55 (100%) already carried (ROWDEM)
    Δp* (inflation) 55/55 (100%) already carried (ROWINF)
    i_s* (short rate) 54/55 (98%) already carried (ROWFIN)
    tc* (total credit) 52/55 (95%) viable — strong candidate
    i_l* (long rate) 39/55 (71%) partial — thin for EMs, redundant with GLB_USLR
    eq* (equity) 4/55 (7%) not viable without a large data build

    (Aggregate coverage is a first-order proxy: what matters per bloc is whether its main trade partners carry the variable. It is close enough to rank the candidates; the trade-weighted per-bloc version is a verification item in §5.)

    3. Per-candidate assessment¶

    tc* — foreign total credit — RECOMMENDED¶

    • 95% coverage (52/55). Missing only PH, RO, US (US carries LEV, a leverage proxy, not a CREDIT_GDP_US level; PH/RO have none). The two-year credit-block expansion put CREDIT_GDP_* on nearly every bloc, so the base is there.
    • No redundancy. GMAPS has no global credit common, so a foreign-credit star adds a genuinely new cross-border credit channel — not a re-aggregation of an existing series.
    • Matches CCFH's own motivation. Total credit (domestic and cross-border) is exactly the variable CCFH add beyond the classical GVAR set "as an important transmission channel of international shocks," and they flag it as decisive over their crisis hold-out. It is the single most defensible extension.
    • One new star.

    i_l* — foreign long rate — DEFER¶

    • 71% coverage (39/55): YIELD10_* for the AEs and euro members; missing 16 (most EMs, the CH/NO/SG financial hubs, and the US — whose long rate is the common GLB_USLR, not a domestic YIELD10_US).
    • Partly redundant with GLB_USLR. Because the US long rate already reaches every bloc as a common, a foreign-long-rate star would re-introduce the US 10y (via the US weight) alongside the commons, risking collinearity; its genuinely new content is the non-US partners' long rates.
    • Thin where it is needed most. For EM-heavy blocs whose partners lack YIELD10, the star would be US-dominated or sparse. Marginal gain for real cost.

    eq* — foreign equity — NOT VIABLE (as-is)¶

    • 7% coverage (only CN, HK, PE, SG carry a domestic equity index; US equity is the common GLB_USEQ, SP500VOL is a volatility series, not a level).
    • Building a real foreign-equity star would first require sourcing ~50 domestic equity indices — a large data effort — and even then it would largely duplicate GLB_USEQ for the many blocs whose only equity signal is the US common. Not recommended without a separate equity-data workstream, and even then of questionable value given the common already carries the dominant global equity factor.

    4. Cost of adding a star (system-wide)¶

    Adding foreign counterparts is not a data-only change — each new star is an endogenous column in every bloc's VARX* and enlarges the coupled state.

    • Coupling dimension grows as N·k·H + 4H with N=38 coupled blocs, H=20, k = #stars. Today k=3 → 2,360.
    • +tc* (k=4): 3,120 (+32%)
    • +tc*+i_l* (k=5): 3,880 (+64%)
    • Pre-computed (I−M)⁻¹ (the shipped operator behind the ~1-second browser solve) scales ≈ O(dim³): the in-browser/precompute step (already ~19 min at dim 2,360, which is why it is pre-shipped) becomes ≈2.3× slower with tc*, ≈4.4× slower with both. Still a one-off, offline cost, but non-trivial.
    • Estimation. Each bloc's VARX* gains 1–2 star regressors. CCFH argue this is fine under shrinkage ("reasonable to include a large set … to avoid ad-hoc restrictions, even for … the US"), and GMAPS's Minnesota-type prior would absorb them the same way — but it still requires a full-system re-estimation (export_bvar_gvar24_loop.m), a coupling-artifact regen, and a shift back to the current quarter.
    • Design tension. GMAPS's sparse-stars-plus-US-commons choice is deliberate (now documented in §2 of the paper). Adding foreign financials (i_l*, eq*) partly duplicates the US commons; adding foreign credit (tc*) does not — which is the cleanest reason to treat tc* differently from the other two.

    5. What to verify before implementing (open items)¶

    1. US contribution to tc* — decide whether US enters the foreign-credit star via LEV, a re-sourced CREDIT_GDP_US, or is dropped from the average (the completed-panel star build must handle PH/RO/US).
    2. Trade-weighted per-bloc coverage — recompute coverage weighted by each bloc's trade shares (not just the aggregate) to confirm tc* is well-defined for every bloc's main partners, and to size how thin i_l* really is for the EM blocs.
    3. Weights choice — CCFH use bilateral-trade weights for all foreign variables but note (citing Eickmeier–Ng) that financial weights (portfolio/credit flows) may be better for financial variables; decide whether tc* should use trade or credit-flow weights.
    4. Collinearity of i_l* with GLB_USLR — if pursued, test empirically whether the foreign long-rate star adds signal beyond the common or merely reintroduces it.
    5. Sequencing — a foreign-counterpart addition is a full-system re-estimation; it should be combined with the JP/KR/TR/TW NEER re-estimation already under way, not run as a second pass.

    6. Recommendation¶

    • Add tc* (foreign total credit) — 95% coverage, no redundancy with the existing commons, matches CCFH's headline extension, one new star, and it is the addition with the clearest economic payoff (a cross-border credit channel GMAPS presently lacks). Fold it into the pending re-estimation.
    • Hold i_l* (foreign long rate) — feasible at 71% but partly redundant with GLB_USLR and thin for EMs; low priority, and only worth it if item 4 shows real incremental signal.
    • Do not add eq* (foreign equity) — 7% coverage; would need a ~50-bloc equity-sourcing workstream and would still largely duplicate GLB_USEQ.

    One-line answer: the only CCFH foreign counterpart GMAPS can add cleanly today is foreign total credit (tc*, 95% coverage, no overlap with the US commons); the foreign long rate is thin and partly redundant, and foreign equity is infeasible without a large new data build and would duplicate the US equity common.

    7. UPDATE — DBnomics / OECD pull: the missing series ARE sourceable¶

    Ran the pull (DBnomics with the provided key, then the live OECD SDMX). This supersedes the "eq* not viable" verdict of §3.

    Equity (eq*). OECD share prices (SPASTT01, "Share prices" index) cover 44 of the 51 blocs that lack a domestic equity series — DE, FR, JP, AU, BR, IN, ZA, KR, MX, and 35 more. Combined with the 4 already carried (CN, HK, PE, SG), that is 48/55 (87%). Still missing after the pull: AR, EA, MY, PH, SA, TH, TW (EA is an aggregate obtainable from its members).

    Long rate (i_l*). OECD long-term rates (IRLTLT01/measure IRLT) cover 10 of the 16 missing — BR, CH, CN, CR, ID, IL, IS, NO, RU, US-as-common. With the 39 already carried, that is 49/55 (89%). Still missing: AR, MY, PH, SA, SG, TR.

    Credit (tc*). Unchanged: 52/55, missing only PH, RO, US.

    Currency. The DBnomics OECD/MEI mirror is frozen at ~2024-01 (OECD deprecated MEI), so it is a history source only. Current data comes from the new OECD SDMX (sdmx.oecd.org), verified serving 2026 data. The pipeline already uses this exact host for long rates (fetch_oecd_irlt.py).

    Correction (2026-07-19, from the build): the equity index level the star needs is NOT in DSD_KEI@DF_KEI — that dataflow serves SHARE only as growth rates. The share-price index level lives in OECD.SDD.STES,DSD_STES@DF_FINMARK,4.0 (SHARE, UNIT=IX), which also carries IRLT (long rate). Both verified live-current (e.g. JPN 2026-06). The euro area resolves directly as the EA20 aggregate — no member-aggregation needed. This is the dataflow the large-model build actually used.

    Revised coverage of the full 6-counterpart mirror:

    counterpart now after OECD pull
    y*, Δp*, i_s* 100 / 100 / 98% (have)
    tc* credit 95% 95%
    i_l* long rate 71% 89%
    eq* equity 7% 87%

    The persistent gaps — blocs still missing a financial after the pull — are the usual thin EMs/hubs: AR, MY, PH, SA miss both equity and long rate; TH, TW miss equity; SG, TR miss long rate; EA needs member-aggregation. This is the same coverage frontier CCFH themselves hit: they report >80% coverage for all variables except long-term rates, "missing for emerging markets with underdeveloped capital markets." So GMAPS at ~87–89% would match, not lag, the comparable.

    Cost, all 3 new stars (6-counterpart mirror). Coupling dimension 38·6·20 + 4·20 = 4{,}640 — from 2,360, so ≈2× the dimension and ≈8× the (I−M)⁻¹ precompute (O(dim³)). One-off, offline; still material.

    8. Revised recommendation¶

    Covering all six is feasible — the data exists for ~87–89% of blocs from a source the pipeline already uses, matching CCFH's own coverage. Concretely:

    1. tc* (credit) — add first, cleanest (95%, no redundancy, matches CCFH's headline extension).
    2. eq* (equity) — now viable via OECD SHARE; the biggest step toward parity with CCFH. Note it will partly co-move with GLB_USEQ, but a trade-weighted foreign equity aggregate is genuinely distinct from the single US equity common.
    3. i_l* (long rate) — viable via OECD IRLT; weigh against its overlap with GLB_USLR (a foreign long-rate star mostly adds the non-US partners' yields).
    4. Leave AR/MY/PH/SA/TH/TW/SG financials thin (same as CCFH's EM gaps); handle EA by member-aggregation.

    Sequencing: all six stars is a full-system re-estimation and a ~2× coupling dimension. It should be one combined pass with the JP/KR/TR/TW NEER re-estimation currently running — not a third re-estimation. The data-sourcing (OECD SHARE/IRLT for the ~54 findable series) can proceed in parallel and offline now.
